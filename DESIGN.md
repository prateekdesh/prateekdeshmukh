# DESIGN.md - Personal Editorial

## The idea

This is Prateek's personal corner of the internet, not an online listing for
hire. Identity, opinions, interests, writing, and humor lead. Engineering work
is one part of the story and stays brief; the detailed resume is linked for
people who want it.

## Content order

1. Opening: name, role, and a direct personal sentence.
2. About: beliefs, opinions, and the way Prateek thinks.
3. Interests: agents, backends, infrastructure, voice AI, distributed systems.
4. Writing: feature the latest post, then link to blog.prateekdeshmukh.com rather
   than duplicating the blog.
5. Work: short essays on Faff, NatWest, and Together Fund. No resume bullets or
   technology inventory.
6. Outside work: reading, chess, running, pickleball, cooking, and food.
7. Contact: direct and personal, without a hiring call to action.

## Visual language

- Spacious editorial layout with strong typography and generous section gaps.
- Warm paper background, dark ink, muted secondary text, and one vermilion
  accent.
- Fraunces for display headings, Newsreader for human prose, Manrope for UI,
  and DM Mono for small labels only.
- Square, quiet controls. No gradients, glows, glass, dark terminal theme, or
  decorative icon system.
- Personality comes from language, occasional pull quotes, and unusual details,
  not from a UI gimmick.

## Voice

- Use Prateek's wording whenever possible and paraphrase minimally.
- Personal first, clever second.
- Let humor be visible but incidental; do not turn the page into a joke format.
- Never invent reflections, opinions, or personal details.
- Avoid recruiter language, inflated claims, generic AI copy, and resume-speak.

## Interaction

- Keep navigation simple and useful.
- Use light scroll reveals only, with a reduced-motion fallback.
- Keep technical depth in the linked resume or the relevant external project.
- Blog content can be integrated through RSS later, but the portfolio should not
  become a second blog.
